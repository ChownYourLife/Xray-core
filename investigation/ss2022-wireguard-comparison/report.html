What was measured

  • Coverage: 5 workloads × 4 frozen products × 7 rounds = 140 cells per protocol, 280 cells total. All applications exited 0. Each cohort used one fixed sequence, no timed retries or post-result retuning. Seven deterministic rotations/reversals are counterbalanced but not perfectly position-balanced (7 is not divisible by 4); workloads rotate by round. TCP and UDP were collected separately, not interleaved.
  • Primary throughput: median of seven round receiver-verified application goodput rates, upload and download separate. Upload is application client → echo receiver; download is echo sender → application client. Units are decimal Mbit/s. No combined direction or cross-protocol score is used.
  • Primary established RTT: median of seven round medians, each over 256 monotonic 64-byte request/verified 64-byte echo RTT samples, with 32 warmup echoes excluded. Measured before throughput, without concurrent throughput. TCP adds a one-byte command to its 64-byte request payload. These are real response times, not ns/op.
  • Fresh first response: separately reported median of seven round medians, each over 8 fresh application dial-through-first-verified-response samples. Initial warmups pre-establish proxy/WireGuard state; this is not a cold WireGuard handshake or merely SOCKS CONNECT timing.
  • Dispersion: every metric shows median followed by Q1–Q3, inclusive linear quartiles over its seven round values. Visible values are rounded to 3 decimals. Exact summary values are retained in numerical data attributes and linked JSON; raw integer nanoseconds and byte counters remain in cell evidence. Pooled 1,792 RTT / 56 setup samples per group do not replace these primary estimators.

Actual routes and CPU roles

Real application, Xray client, candidate relay, WireGuard gateway and echo processes ran in private Linux network namespaces on 216.195.211.248. Standalone classic SS uses chacha20-ietf-poly1305; standalone SS2022 uses 2022-blake3-aes-128-gcm, both candidate inbound → freedom → echo. The other three paths are classic SS, VLESS or VMess inbound → candidate WireGuard outbound → fixed real WireGuard gateway → echo. VLESS uses encryption none over plain TCP without TLS/XTLS/mux; VMess uses AEAD chacha20-poly1305 over plain TCP without TLS/mux.

The WireGuard inner virtual destination is 10.77.0.1:19090; gateway userspace netstack forwards by freedom redirect to 127.0.0.1:19090. Actual route and gateway-removal controls passed; the virtual destination was not a namespace host route. The candidate relay is assigned CPUs 2,3 with GOMAXPROCS=2. Fixed client, gateway, measurement and echo share CPUs 0,1 with GOMAXPROCS=2; that support contention may cap end-to-end performance. Production affinity was untouched. UDP: separate loss-aware application workload

Not raw UDP capacity. This is a windowed loss-aware application workload with reliable control through the same Xray route and an exact 100 ms receiver drain/cutoff. Its goodput includes drain and control/barrier overhead. It is not unpaced UDP capacity and must not be compared with TCP capacity.

Each direction offers 16,742,400 payload bytes: 436 windows × 32 datagrams × 1,200 bytes. There are zero DATA retransmissions. Transfer IDs, sequence numbers and payload bytes are verified; unique verified delivery, loss, duplicates and corruption are retained, not hidden by an all-packets-arrived assumption. Per-window reliable TCP progress (upload) or pull (download) responses do not assert UDP arrival. This separate control connection follows the same actual application/Xray route.

After the final reliable upload Q request arrives, the receiver drains for exactly 100 ms; after the final download D response, the client receiver drains for exactly 100 ms. Receiver locking orders counter observation, cutoff and final snapshot; observations at or after cutoff are excluded. Final receiver counters are reliably round-tripped. Goodput uses unique verified payload bytes divided by receiver first-data-to-fixed-cutoff monotonic duration. Random transfer registration and 32 real 64-byte warmup echoes precede measurement. For VLESS/VMess, UDP forwarding travels through their raw TCP stream; SS data uses UDP transport.

Loss columns are the median of seven per-round loss rates, by direction, from the primary UDP summary—not an aggregate loss percentage. For example, official SS2022 upload is 12.413990825688073% median loss; the separately computed aggregate is approximately 12.68635%, a different statistic. All metrics below retain Q1–Q3 over the seven round values.

Limitations and host-preservation boundary

  • Whole-product code and dependency differences prevent attributing observed changes solely to batching/GSO. The fixed latest-hybrid support nodes also make this a relay-only product substitution, not four fully homogeneous deployments.
  • Same-host IPv4 private-loopback isolation removes a real WAN path. CPU sharing, support endpoint limits and cohort ordering remain part of the observation. Results establish neither internet capacity nor internet latency and do not identify the internal location of UDP drops.
  • Network namespace, host IPv6 sysctls and IPv4/IPv6 routes were unchanged. UDP running-service snapshots were unchanged. TCP's exact running-service comparison was false: fwupd.service disappeared between passive snapshots (31 to 30 listed units). Its cause is unobserved; do not infer benchmark causality or claim every service remained unchanged.
  • No service, package, host route or host sysctl modifications were made; no production source edits or commits. Current cohort evidence did not capture manual/container Xray PID/command identity before and after. Historical PID evidence is not a contemporaneous preservation check.
  • Q1–Q3 is observed seven-round dispersion, not a confidence interval. Three-decimal display rounding is not additional measurement precision. No pooled latency substitute or combined TCP/UDP score is presented.

  • TCP: 128 MiB (134,217,728 bytes) verified payload per direction, with 8 MiB warmup per direction excluded. Receiver-ready-to-final-verified-byte timing and deterministic payload checks are unchanged from the original final TCP driver.
  • Established RTT: 256 real 64-byte verified echoes per round after 32 warmups, measured before throughput without concurrent throughput. Fresh first response: 8 fresh-flow samples per round; pre-established support/WireGuard state is not a cold WireGuard handshake. Primary latency is the median of seven round medians, never the pooled request median.
  • UDP: 16,742,400 offered payload bytes per direction (436 × 32 × 1,200 bytes), zero DATA retransmissions. Same-route separate reliable TCP control carries progress/pull barriers and final receiver counters without assuming all UDP arrived. Upload cutoff is exactly 100 ms after the receiver's final reliable Q receipt; download cutoff is exactly 100 ms after the client's final D response. Receiver locking excludes observations at/after cutoff. Goodput is unique verified bytes divided by first-data-to-fixed-cutoff receiver duration, including control/barrier and drain overhead; this is not raw UDP capacity.
  • Every primary metric uses the median of seven round values, inclusive linear Q1–Q3, and median absolute deviation (MAD). Display rounding is three decimals; exact values are in attributes and summaries. The expandable tables retain exact min/max/MAD. Quartiles are dispersion, not confidence intervals.
